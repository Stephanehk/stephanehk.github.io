Stance Weights: Opus experts, updated prompt

For each rater, two logistic regressions on the 31 situation features, for the action and the framing stance. The first predicts whether the rater takes a position (explicit refutation, indirect pushback, praise or confirmation) or not (take no position, not applicable). The second uses only the vignettes where the rater took a position, and predicts whether that stance is affirming (praise, confirmation) or challenging (explicit refutation, indirect pushback). Weights are per standard deviation of the feature.

positive weight: more likely to take a position, or to affirmnegative weight: less likely● bold: bootstrap 95% interval excludes 0

Where Flash's weights differ most

Experts here are Opus with the updated prompt, on the same 100 vignettes and Flash replies as the other pages. Each difference below is Flash's weight minus the average of the schools that could be modeled, and its paired bootstrap interval excludes 0. Read them with the accuracy of each school's model in mind: whether a school takes a position is mostly not predictable from the situation on these 100 vignettes (action: MI p .16, CBT p .79, ACT p .13, SFBT p .18; framing: only CBT is predictable, p .004). The direction of a stance, once taken, is predictable for CBT, ACT and SFBT on the action (all p .002).

Taking a position

Action stance

  • User asks for a judgment: Flash +0.83, schools +0.04 (difference +0.79). Flash becomes more likely to take a stance; the schools on average don't (CBT, ACT and SFBT individually differ from Flash).
  • User's sense of loss: Flash −0.97, schools −0.23 (difference −0.74). User's sense they can cope: Flash −0.79, schools −0.05 (difference −0.74). Flash steps back from a stance in both cases much more than the schools do.
  • How much attention the situation demands of the user: Flash +0.51, schools −0.12 (difference +0.62).
  • User asks for writing: Flash −0.42, schools +0.09 (difference −0.51).

Framing stance

  • User's sense of threat: Flash −0.54, schools +0.22 (difference −0.76). Loss: Flash −0.70, schools +0.03 (difference −0.73). Flash avoids a stance on the framing when the situation is threatening or costly; the schools don't.
  • User asks for writing: Flash −1.03, schools −0.37 (difference −0.66). Same direction as the schools, stronger.
  • The matter conflicts with the user's own standards: Flash +0.06, schools −0.53 (difference +0.59).

Affirming vs challenging, where a position is taken

On the action, Flash and the schools mostly agree on what sets the direction: the more the user is in the wrong, the more likely a challenge (Flash −1.07; CBT −1.21, ACT −1.16, SFBT −0.70, MI −0.64). Two differences: when the situation is threatening for the user, Flash leans towards challenging (−0.43) and the schools don't (+0.06); on writing requests, everyone leans towards affirming, Flash more (+0.72 vs +0.26).

On the framing, none of the models predicts better than chance (Flash p .13, CBT .35, ACT .25, SFBT .13), so the differences there (judgment requests: Flash −0.60, schools +0.03; the user's own standards: Flash +0.66, schools −0.01) are weak.

Read these as leads: 100 vignettes, 31 features, intervals not corrected for multiple comparisons. The Flash judge labels were made before the judge's "lead with" definition was updated.
