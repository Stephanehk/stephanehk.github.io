Stance Weights: Opus experts, updated prompt

For each rater, two logistic regressions on the 31 situation features, for the action and the framing stance. The first predicts whether the rater takes a position (explicit refutation, indirect pushback, praise or confirmation) or not (take no position, not applicable). The second uses only the vignettes where the rater took a position, and predicts whether that stance is affirming (praise, confirmation) or challenging (explicit refutation, indirect pushback). Weights are per standard deviation of the feature.

positive weight: more likely to take a position, or to affirmnegative weight: less likely● bold: bootstrap 95% interval excludes 0

Takeaways

Experts: Opus with the updated prompt. Flash judge: the updated judge prompt (same definitions as the experts). 100 evaluation vignettes.

  • Flash informs or suggests more than every school. It opens with suggestions or information on 95 of 100. The closest schools are CBT (58) and MI (51); ACT is at 43, SFBT 24, person-centered 19.
  • Flash takes a stance on the action more than every school. Flash 80; CBT 64, ACT 47, SFBT 46, MI 29, person-centered 1.
  • On the framing, Flash takes a stance about as often as CBT, but in the opposite direction more often. Flash 85, CBT 78, ACT 69, MI 35, SFBT 30, person-centered 0. CBT and ACT mostly push back on the framing (69 and 58 times); Flash affirms it 39 times and challenges it 46. Where CBT pushes back on the framing, Flash confirms it on 20 vignettes; for ACT, 16.
  • Only Flash refutes explicitly: 13 times on the action, 15 on the framing. No school does either.
  • On the action, Flash rarely goes the opposite way to a school. It confirms where a school challenges on at most 2 vignettes per school. Its departures are almost all taking a stance where the school takes none.

Where Flash's weights differ most

Each difference is Flash's weight minus the average of the schools that could be modeled, listed when its paired bootstrap interval excludes 0. Whether a school takes a position is mostly not predictable from the situation on these 100 vignettes (only CBT's framing choice is, p .004), so these are differences in how strongly Flash responds to a feature, not a full account of the schools.

Taking a position

  • Action: when the user feels able to cope, Flash backs off much more (−0.83 vs −0.05). When the user asks for a judgment, Flash takes a stance much more (+0.80 vs +0.04). On writing requests Flash takes a stance less (−0.37 vs +0.09).
  • Framing: Flash avoids a stance when the user has suffered a loss (−0.96 vs +0.03; every school differs individually) or feels threatened (−0.50 vs +0.22), and on writing requests (−0.92 vs −0.37). It takes a stance more when the user wants to understand themselves or others (+0.48 vs +0.07).

Affirming vs challenging, once a stance is taken

  • Action: Flash and the schools follow the LM verdict in the same way (the more the user is wrong, the more likely a challenge). On writing requests, all affirm more, Flash most (+0.74 vs +0.26).
  • Framing: when the user asks for a judgment, Flash leans towards challenging the framing (−0.70) and the schools don't (+0.03). When the matter conflicts with the user's own standards, Flash leans towards affirming (+0.73 vs −0.01).

Leads, not conclusions: 100 vignettes, 31 features, intervals not corrected for multiple comparisons.
