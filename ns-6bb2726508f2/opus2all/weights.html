Stance Weights: Opus experts, all 578 vignettes

For each rater, two logistic regressions on the 31 situation features, for the action and the framing stance. The first predicts whether the rater takes a position (explicit refutation, indirect pushback, praise or confirmation) or not (take no position, not applicable). The second uses only the vignettes where the rater took a position, and predicts whether that stance is affirming (praise, confirmation) or challenging (explicit refutation, indirect pushback). Takeaways

Experts: Opus with the updated prompt. Flash judge: updated judge prompt. All 578 vignettes; 576 have Flash labels (101-151: no Flash response, blocked by Gemini; 101-90: judge prompt blocked). Percentages are of 576.

What Flash does differently

  • Flash informs or suggests more than every school. It opens with suggestions or information on 93%. CBT does so on 44%, MI 43%, ACT 32%, SFBT 13%, person-centered 9%.
  • Flash takes a stance on the action more than every school: 80%, against CBT 62%, MI 22%, ACT 42%, SFBT 41%, person-centered 0%.
  • On the framing, Flash takes a stance about as often as CBT (91% vs 85%), but affirms far more. Flash affirms the user's framing on 38% of vignettes; the schools on 3–11%. Where CBT pushes back on the framing, Flash confirms it on 140 vignettes; for ACT, 122; for MI, 43.
  • Only Flash refutes explicitly: on 7% of actions and 13% of framings. No school ever does.
  • On the action, Flash's departures are about whether to take a stance, not which way. Against each school, Flash confirms where the school challenges on at most 29 vignettes (CBT), and takes a stance where the school takes none on 152–457.

When Flash takes a stance the experts withhold

Among vignettes where at least 3 of 5 schools take no position (action 404, framing 310), Flash takes a stance on 76% and 87%. The strongest driver is what the user asks for (all q < .01):

  • Judgment requests: Flash takes a stance on 98% (action) and 100% (framing), against 66% and 80% otherwise.
  • Requests to understand themselves or others: on the framing, Flash takes a stance on 100% (76 of 76).
  • Writing requests: Flash holds back more, taking a stance on 53% (action) and 55% (framing), against 80% and 97% otherwise.
  • With all five schools holding back (action 171 vignettes, framing 53), the same three goal effects hold, and on the action Flash also departs less in deliberation (59% vs 81%) and more in evaluation (84% vs 64%).
  • The user's sense of loss and ability to cope, which looked decisive on the first 100 vignettes, matter much less on all 576 (q 0.14–0.26).

Regressions (all schools except person-centered are now predictable, p ≤ .02)

  • Taking a position: Flash responds to the request type far more than the schools. On judgment requests it takes a stance on the action much more (+0.93 vs −0.08) and on writing requests much less (−0.45 vs +0.37); on the framing, writing requests −1.59 vs −0.22, judgment +0.96 vs +0.05, understanding +1.00 vs +0.22. Every school differs from Flash individually on these.
  • Affirming vs challenging the action: Flash and the schools follow the LM verdict (Flash −1.07, schools −1.42). Flash affirms more in planning and less in evaluation than the schools.
  • Affirming vs challenging the framing: when the user wants to understand themselves or others, the schools lean towards challenging the framing (−0.87) and Flash doesn't (+0.02). When the user feels able to cope, the schools affirm the framing (+0.88) much more than Flash (+0.14). On judgment requests Flash leans towards challenging (−0.38) and the schools towards affirming (+0.25).
