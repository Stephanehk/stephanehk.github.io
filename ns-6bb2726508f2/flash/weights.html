Stance Weights

For each rater, two logistic regressions on the 31 situation features, for the action and the framing stance. The first predicts whether the rater takes a position (explicit refutation, indirect pushback, praise or confirmation) or not (take no position, not applicable). The second uses only the vignettes where the rater took a position, and predicts whether that stance is affirming (praise, confirmation) or challenging (explicit refutation, indirect pushback). Weights are per standard deviation of the feature.

positive weight: more likely to take a position, or to affirmnegative weight: less likely● bold: bootstrap 95% interval excludes 0

How well each model fits

Cross-validated AUC (0.5 = chance, 0.7 acceptable, 0.8 good, 0.9 excellent) and McFadden pseudo-R² (0.2–0.4 is a good fit for a logistic model). Models with AUC below 0.7 are marked weak: they beat chance on the permutation test but the situation features explain little of the choice, so their weights below are faded.

Takeaways: Gemini 3.8 Flash

576 vignettes; experts: Opus, updated prompt; current judge (carrying out the user's request counts as implicit confirmation); design situation labels.

  • Flash suggests or informs more than every school: it opens with suggestions or information on 92%; CBT 44%, MI 43%, ACT 32%, SFBT 13%, person-centered 9%.
  • Flash takes a stance on the user's action more than every school: 88%, against CBT 62%, ACT 42%, SFBT 41%, MI 22%, person-centered 0%.
  • On the framing, Flash takes a stance more often than every school (99% against CBT 85%), and affirms far more. Flash confirms the user's framing on 48% of vignettes; the schools on 3–11%. Where a school pushes back on the framing, Flash confirms it on 173 vignettes against CBT, 146 against ACT and 52 against MI.
  • Only Flash refutes explicitly: 7% of actions and 15% of framings; no school ever does.
  • On the action, Flash's departures are about taking a stance at all, not which way. Opposite-direction cases: at most 56 vignettes (against CBT). Taking a stance where the school takes none: 169 (CBT) to 504 (person-centered). Once a stance is taken, its direction follows the LM verdict as the schools' does (Flash −1.07; schools −1.21 to −1.77).

Where no expert takes a stance on the action (171 vignettes; Flash takes one on 77%)

  • When the interaction goal is "the user is asking whether something is right, wrong, appropriate, reasonable, or justified…", Flash is more likely to take a stance on the action (96% vs 68%; q < 0.01).
  • When the interaction goal is "the user is asking what a person (themselves or someone else) feels, means, intends, or why they act that way", Flash is less likely to (61% vs 88%; q < 0.01).
  • When the action phase is "the user has not acted yet, but is weighing whether or how to act and has not decided", Flash is less likely to (60% vs 87%; q < 0.01).
  • When the LM verdict is not applicable (nothing the user did could be judged), Flash is less likely to (22% vs 80%; q = 0.01).

Where no expert takes a stance on the framing (53 vignettes), Flash takes one on 98%, so no situation distinguishes when it does.
