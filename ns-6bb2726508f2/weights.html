Stance Weights

For each rater, two logistic regressions on the 31 situation features, for the action and the framing stance. The first predicts whether the rater takes a position (explicit refutation, indirect pushback, praise or confirmation) or not (take no position, not applicable). The second uses only the vignettes where the rater took a position, and predicts whether that stance is affirming (praise, confirmation) or challenging (explicit refutation, indirect pushback). Weights are per standard deviation of the feature.

positive weight: more likely to take a position, or to affirmnegative weight: less likely● bold: bootstrap 95% interval excludes 0

Where Flash's weights differ most

Taking a position

Each difference below is Flash's weight minus the average weight of the four schools that could be modeled. All are differences whose paired bootstrap interval excludes 0, listed by size. The largest differences come from two kinds of feature: what the user asks for, and how hard the situation is for the user. Flash's decision to take a position moves with these, and on average the schools' decisions move much less, or the other way.

Action stance

  • User asks for a judgment: Flash +0.83, schools −0.10 (difference +0.93). Flash becomes more likely to take a stance on the action when asked "was I right?". SFBT becomes less likely (−0.60), and MI moves with Flash more weakly (+0.39).
  • User's sense they can cope with the problem: Flash −0.79, schools −0.02 (difference −0.78). Flash steps back when the user already feels able to deal with it; the schools don't respond.
  • User asks for writing: Flash −0.42, schools +0.30 (difference −0.72). On drafts, ACT (+0.38) and SFBT (+0.60) become more likely to take a stance on the action, and Flash less likely.
  • User's sense of loss: Flash −0.97, schools −0.31 (difference −0.66). Both step back as loss rises; Flash much more.
  • Smaller differences: the user's sense that others control the situation (Flash +0.57, schools −0.04), how much attention the situation demands of the user (+0.51 vs −0.09), how pleasant the situation is for the user (−0.26 vs +0.24), and the LM rating the user's control as higher than the user does (+0.23 vs −0.24).

Framing stance

  • User's sense of loss: Flash −0.70, schools +0.07 (difference −0.77). This is the only feature where Flash differs from each of the four schools individually.
  • User's sense of threat: Flash −0.54, schools +0.18 (difference −0.71).
  • User asks for writing: Flash −1.03, schools −0.44 (difference −0.59). Same direction as the schools, but much stronger.
  • User blames themselves: Flash +0.44, schools −0.12 (difference +0.56). User blames others: Flash +0.40, schools −0.02 (difference +0.42).

Affirming vs challenging, where a position is taken

Fewer raters can be modeled here, because some almost always stance in one direction. On the action, MI affirms in 25 of its 32 stances and SFBT in 49 of 55, so only CBT and ACT are compared with Flash. On the action stance, Flash and the schools mostly agree on what decides the direction. For CBT (−1.04), ACT (−0.99) and Flash (−1.07) alike, the strongest driver is the LM verdict: the more the user is in the wrong, the more likely a challenge. Two differences stand out:

  • User's sense of control over the situation: Flash −0.21, CBT and ACT +0.29 (difference −0.50). When the user feels in control, the two schools lean towards affirming and Flash leans slightly towards challenging.
  • User asks for writing: Flash +0.72, CBT and ACT +0.41 (difference +0.31). All three are more likely to affirm the plan behind a draft; Flash more so.

On the framing stance, only SFBT predicts the direction better than chance (MI p .79, CBT p .75, ACT p .10, Flash p .13), so the differences there say little. For what they are worth: when the user asks for a judgment, Flash leans towards challenging the user's framing (−0.60) more than the schools on average (−0.08), and when the user blames others, Flash leans towards affirming (+0.43, schools −0.14).

Read these as leads. The schools' average includes three models that predict no better than chance (CBT on action; MI and SFBT on framing). The intervals are not corrected for testing 31 features, so one or two of the differences above may be chance. The per-school differences are in lm_eval/out/weights.json.
