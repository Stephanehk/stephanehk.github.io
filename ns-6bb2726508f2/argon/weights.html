Stance Weights: Argon experts

For each rater, two logistic regressions on the 31 situation features, for the action and the framing stance. The first predicts whether the rater takes a position (explicit refutation, indirect pushback, praise or confirmation) or not (take no position, not applicable). The second uses only the vignettes where the rater took a position, and predicts whether that stance is affirming (praise, confirmation) or challenging (explicit refutation, indirect pushback). Weights are per standard deviation of the feature.

positive weight: more likely to take a position, or to affirmnegative weight: less likely● bold: bootstrap 95% interval excludes 0

What these weights can and can't show

The experts on this page are Gemini 4 Argon with the updated expert prompt, on the same 100 vignettes and the same Flash responses as the Opus page. Argon almost never takes a position on the action or the framing for person-centered (0 of 100), MI (1 of 100) or ACT (7 of 100 on action, 11 on framing). Those schools can't be modeled, so only CBT and SFBT appear in the tables. The schools' mean in the last column is therefore over one or two schools, not four.

  • Taking a position on the action: only SFBT's choice is predictable from the situation (accuracy 0.79). It takes a position (always praise) less often the more the user is in the wrong (−0.95) and in the deliberation phase (−0.86). CBT's choice is not distinguishable from chance (p 0.12).
  • Taking a position on the framing: no school's choice is predictable (CBT p 0.08, ACT p 0.14, SFBT p 0.81).
  • Affirming vs challenging: only CBT on the action can be modeled, and it is not predictable (p 0.48). SFBT's 46 action stances are all praise.

With these experts, most of the difference from Flash is that Flash takes a position and the experts don't, so the per-feature differences say little. Compare with the Opus version of this page.
